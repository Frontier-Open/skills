# Frontier Signals editorial playbook

## Reader promise

Help Chinese founders, builders, investors, creators, and knowledge workers understand one consequential change in AI or technology and decide what to notice or do next.

## Mode decision

Choose the shortest mode that can support the evidence:

| Mode | Use when | Length | Minimum sources | Production visuals | Shape |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `quick` | One event or a tight cluster has an immediate, defensible implication | 1,400–2,200 Chinese characters | 3 | 3–5 | event → judgment → mechanism → implication |
| `deep` | A structural question needs history, competing explanations, and observable scenarios | 3,200–5,000 | 6 | 5–8 | question → context → evidence → counterargument → implication → watchlist |

Use `quick` by default. Do not call an article `deep` merely because it is long. Count production visuals as the WeChat cover, web/OG visual, and inline section images; this is a production-asset requirement, not permission to add decorative filler. The media purpose test remains mandatory.

## Selection

Score candidates as judgment support, not fake precision:

- reader relevance: 30%;
- consequence or structural importance: 25%;
- freshness and momentum: 15%;
- evidence quality: 15%;
- originality of the proposed angle: 15%.

Prefer a strong slower-moving question over a weak breaking-news summary. Default to a 72-hour news window; use older sources only for context.

Select only candidates that answer all five questions:

1. What changed in the last 24–72 hours?
2. Why does it matter to a Chinese founder, builder, investor, creator, or knowledge worker?
3. What verified hard fact anchors the article?
4. What tension, mechanism, or second-order consequence is being missed?
5. Which two or three future observations could confirm or weaken the thesis?

Hotness is discovery input, not the thesis. Reject a ten-item roundup disguised as an article.

## Thesis test

A viable thesis:

1. says what changed;
2. explains the mechanism, not just the outcome;
3. identifies who gains, loses, or must adapt;
4. can be challenged by evidence;
5. is not interchangeable with “AI is changing everything.”

Write the thesis before drafting. If the evidence does not support it, change the thesis rather than stretching the facts.

## Title

Draft exactly three candidates before selecting the final title:

- `factual`: subject + verified change + consequence;
- `judgment`: familiar event + overlooked mechanism;
- `question`: rising tension + a question the article actually answers.

Aim for 18–30 Chinese characters and one punctuation break when possible. Use one of these honest structures:

- concrete change + unexpected consequence;
- familiar event + overlooked mechanism;
- rising tension + clear object;
- direct question only when the article genuinely answers it.

Avoid empty superlatives, fabricated urgency, “所有人都…”, “彻底颠覆”, “刚刚”, “突发”, “炸裂”, and withholding the subject merely to force a click. Do not turn correlation into causation or use “全球第一”, “首次”, or “颠覆” without primary evidence and a clear scope.

For every candidate, record `claim_check`: the exact evidence required to make the title true. Choose a title only after verifying that the supporting evidence appears within the first 20% of the article.

## Opening

Write `hook` as three parts before composing the visible intro:

1. `event`: the verified change;
2. `tension`: the contradiction or unresolved stakes;
3. `judgment`: the article thesis in plain Chinese.

The first 120–180 Chinese characters must contain:

1. a concrete fact, scene, or contradiction;
2. why the reader should care now;
3. the article’s central judgment.

Reach the thesis on the first screen. A `deep` article may open with a person or scene, but introduce a hard fact or the central contradiction within 250 characters. Do not begin with brand introduction, a generic history of AI, or “在这个快速变化的时代”.

## Argument structure

- Give each section one job and an informative title that can be understood on its own.
- Build each section as judgment → evidence → mechanism → implication. Do not restate the headline in every section.
- Keep `quick` to 3 sections by default; use a fourth only when it prevents evidence and implication from being conflated.
- Use 4–6 sections for `deep`. Include a rendered `counterargument` section that states the strongest credible objection, supporting evidence, and the boundary or observation that would make the thesis wrong.
- Keep most paragraphs between 40 and 90 Chinese characters for mobile rhythm. Use one idea and one to three sentences per paragraph; normally stay below 120 characters.
- Introduce a semantic section, evidence visual, or other meaningful pause every 400–700 characters. Do not split every sentence into its own paragraph merely to create white space.
- Use pull quotes only for original judgments worth remembering.
- Use lists only when the information is genuinely parallel or actionable.

Mark the function of each section in `role`: `context`, `evidence`, `mechanism`, `counterargument`, `implication`, or `watchlist`.

## Sources and fact checks

Follow [source-and-fact-checking.md](source-and-fact-checking.md). Use primary material first, then independent reporting and specialist analysis. A source minimum is not a diversity guarantee: several articles repeating one unnamed source count as one chain.

Create one `fact_check` item for every material claim. Record:

- the claim in neutral language;
- whether it is `fact`, `attributed-reporting`, `interpretation`, or `recommendation`;
- supporting source IDs;
- `verified` or explicitly bounded `qualified` status;
- whether it is high risk and how many independent sources support it;
- a short note covering units, time window, uncertainty, or attribution.

Consequential, disputed, reputation-sensitive, market-moving, safety, financing, or personnel claims require at least two independent sources. Timestamp volatile metrics. A source list at the end does not replace nearby attribution in the prose.

## Visual plan

Prioritize visuals in this order:

1. evidence: original chart, filing excerpt, paper figure, or product screenshot;
2. explanation: verified timeline, relationship map, or original diagram;
3. scene: relevant portrait, place, or product view with usable rights;
4. atmosphere: one original editorial illustration at most.

Use 3–5 production visuals for `quick` and 5–8 for `deep`, counting cover, OG, and inline section images. With the current renderer, use 1–3 inline images for `quick` and 3–6 for `deep`. Every image must answer “what information does this add?” Record purpose, local path, alt text, caption, credit, source URL, and rights status. Label AI-generated visuals; never present them as documentary evidence. Include units, denominator, and time range on charts.

## Voice

Write with calm conviction: sharp, curious, future-facing, and concrete. Frontier Signals may be witty, but it is never breathless, preachy, or cute. Explain technical details in plain language without pretending uncertainty has disappeared.

## Ending

End in this order:

1. return to the thesis without repeating the introduction;
2. state the practical implication for the target reader;
3. name two or three observable `watchlist` indicators;
4. optionally ask one specific question that follows from the argument.

Do not end with a generic request to like, share, or follow. Channel-specific calls to action belong outside the canonical article body.

## Final edit

- Delete the first paragraph if it only warms up.
- Replace vague nouns such as “趋势” or “生态” with the actual mechanism.
- Check whether each number changes the argument; remove decorative metrics.
- Read every section title in sequence. They should reveal the logic of the article.
- Confirm the title makes a promise the conclusion actually fulfills.
- Confirm `reader_payoff` states what the reader can understand, notice, or do differently.
- Confirm every material claim appears in `fact_check` and every cited source is actually used.
- Confirm every high-risk claim has two independent sources or has been removed.
- Confirm `quick` and `deep` length, source, visual, section, and reading-time budgets.
- Confirm a `deep` article contains the strongest credible counterargument in the rendered body.
- Confirm the final title is one of `title_candidates` and is supported within the first 20%.
- Confirm every visual passes the purpose and rights tests; never add filler to reach a count.
- Confirm facts, attributed reporting, interpretation, and recommendation are distinguishable.
- Confirm the ending includes a practical implication and two or three observable indicators, not a generic engagement prompt.

## Avoided patterns

- unrelated news lists presented as analysis;
- emotional language substituting for evidence;
- conclusions chosen before research;
- correlation written as causation;
- sourceless charts or screenshots;
- generic AI robots, glowing brains, or decorative space imagery;
- heavy internet slang that weakens the durable Frontier Signals voice;
- translation-like prose, filler abstractions, and ornamental bolding or color;
- a one-sentence-per-paragraph rhythm throughout the entire article;
- calls to action inserted into the canonical editorial body.
